# Creative Asset Quality Review

## Purpose
Evaluate supplied creative assets against their communication objective, audience, brand, factual accuracy, visual/audio quality, accessibility and platform requirements. Give specific, prioritised improvements and distinguish visible evidence from subjective judgement.

## When to use
Use to review images, videos, advertisements, social posts, thumbnails, presentations, voiceovers, audio mixes or campaign creative before publication or delivery.

## Required inputs
- Actual asset or accessible preview
- Objective, audience and intended action
- Platform, placement and technical specifications
- Brand guide and approved assets
- Required message, claims, disclosures and source material
- Known production constraints and review priorities

## Instructions
1. Confirm which asset and version are being reviewed, the intended audience, placement and desired outcome.
2. Inspect only the material actually supplied or accessible. State if a file, frame, audio track or detail cannot be examined; never claim to have seen or heard unavailable content.
3. Evaluate message clarity and hierarchy. Identify the first thing a viewer/listener notices, the core takeaway and whether the intended action is obvious.
4. Review audience and platform fit: format, aspect ratio, duration, crop, safe zones, mobile legibility, pacing and interface overlays.
5. Check brand consistency against the supplied guide: logo integrity, colours, typography, tone, spacing and prohibited elements. Do not invent brand rules.
6. Check factual and claim accuracy against supplied evidence. Flag unsupported statistics, promises, dates, locations, testimonials, imagery or implied claims.
7. For visual assets, assess composition, contrast, typography, readability, visual balance, image quality, consistency and accessibility.
8. For video, assess opening hook, narrative flow, shot continuity, pacing, caption accuracy, audio clarity, transitions and ending.
9. For audio, assess intelligibility, pacing, pronunciation, levels, clipping, noise, music balance and rights/disclosure requirements where evidence is available.
10. Separate objective defects from creative preferences. Explain the audience or performance consequence of each material issue.
11. Rank fixes as critical, high, medium or optional. For each, state the exact issue, why it matters and a practical correction.
12. Provide a final readiness judgement: ready, ready with minor changes, revise before publishing or unable to assess. State the evidence behind the judgement.

## Expected output
- Asset/version and review scope
- Overall readiness judgement
- Strengths worth preserving
- Findings by message, audience, brand, craft, accessibility and platform fit
- Factual/claim checks and evidence gaps
- Prioritised fixes with suggested corrections
- Final pre-publication checklist
- Limitations of the review

## Quality checks
- Assessment is grounded in the supplied asset
- Unseen or inaudible details are not invented
- Brand standards are based on supplied guidance
- Factual claims are checked or clearly flagged
- Critical issues are distinguished from subjective preferences
- Recommendations are specific and actionable
- Accessibility and platform constraints are considered
- Readiness judgement follows from the findings
