# Clear Brief Writer

## Purpose
Turn rough notes, messages or incomplete instructions into a concise working brief that gives a recipient enough context to act. Preserve the source meaning, expose missing information and never invent facts, approvals or commitments.

## When to use
Use when preparing a project brief, creative brief, task request, agency instruction, internal assignment or handover from scattered source material.

## Required inputs
- Original notes, messages or source documents
- Intended recipient and their role
- Desired outcome and decision/action required
- Known context, constraints and deadline
- Required deliverables and acceptance criteria, if known
- Preferred length and tone

## Instructions
1. Read all supplied material and identify the requested outcome, intended audience and action the recipient must take.
2. Extract explicit facts, decisions, requirements, constraints, dates, owners and dependencies. Preserve exact names, figures and terminology.
3. Separate confirmed information from assumptions, interpretations, open questions and conflicting statements.
4. Identify missing information that could materially change the work. Ask only essential clarification questions; if work can proceed, list assumptions clearly.
5. Organise the brief in this order where relevant: title/context, objective, audience, problem or opportunity, key facts, scope, deliverables, requirements, exclusions, deadline/milestones, responsibilities, dependencies, acceptance criteria and open questions.
6. Convert vague requests into observable deliverables or acceptance criteria without changing the intent. Mark proposed criteria as suggestions rather than agreed requirements.
7. Remove repetition, ambiguity and irrelevant detail. Use plain language, active verbs and scannable headings.
8. Check that every instruction is actionable and that no unsupported owner, budget, date, approval or promise has been added.
9. Provide a short list of unresolved decisions and the next action needed to finalise the brief.

## Expected output
- Brief title and context
- Objective and intended audience
- Scope and key requirements
- Deliverables and proposed acceptance criteria
- Constraints, exclusions and dependencies
- Timeline/owners only where supplied
- Open questions and stated assumptions
- Immediate next action

## Quality checks
- Original intent and critical details are preserved
- Facts, assumptions and proposals are clearly distinguished
- No invented commitments, owners, dates or figures
- Deliverables are specific enough to act on
- Scope and exclusions are clear
- Missing information is visible without overwhelming the brief
- Language is concise and understandable to the recipient
