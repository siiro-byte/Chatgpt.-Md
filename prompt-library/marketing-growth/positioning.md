# Positioning & Messaging

## Purpose
Define a credible, differentiated market position by connecting a specific audience need to a relevant offer and evidence customers can trust.

## When to use
Use for brand or product positioning, repositioning, offer refinement, launch messaging and decisions about why customers should choose one offer over alternatives.

## Required inputs
- Brand, product or service
- Target market and priority audience
- Customer needs, use cases and buying context
- Competitors, substitutes and current workarounds
- Customer research, sales evidence and feedback
- Business objective, constraints and channels

## Instructions
1. Define the positioning decision, market scope and intended customer.
2. Describe the audience through needs, behaviour, use cases and decision criteria. Avoid unsupported demographic assumptions.
3. Review customer evidence for problems, desired outcomes, objections, switching barriers and purchase triggers. Label evidence gaps.
4. Map direct competitors, indirect substitutes, workarounds and the option of doing nothing.
5. Compare alternatives using criteria customers value, not internal feature lists alone.
6. Identify the offer's relevant advantages. Separate features, customer benefits and proof that the benefits are delivered.
7. Draft a positioning statement: For [audience] in [situation], [offer] is the [category/frame] that [distinctive value], because [reason to believe]. Label unverified elements as hypotheses.
8. Build a message hierarchy: core promise, supporting messages, proof points and evidence-based responses to objections.
9. Produce concise value proposition and headline examples suited to the requested channel.
10. Check for generic claims, unsupported superlatives, imitation and promises the organisation cannot fulfil.
11. Recommend practical validation tests such as customer interviews, message comprehension checks, landing-page tests or sales-call analysis. State the signal that would change the position.

## Expected output
- Positioning objective and market scope
- Priority audience and customer need
- Alternatives and customer decision criteria
- Differentiation and supporting evidence
- Positioning statement
- Core promise and supporting message hierarchy
- Proof points and objection responses
- Sample value proposition/headlines
- Validation plan and evidence gaps

## Quality checks
- Audience and buying situation are specific
- Differentiation matters to customers
- Claims are supported or labelled as hypotheses
- Alternatives include indirect options and status quo
- Promise is credible and deliverable
- Messaging is clear and consistent
- Validation tests can change a real decision
