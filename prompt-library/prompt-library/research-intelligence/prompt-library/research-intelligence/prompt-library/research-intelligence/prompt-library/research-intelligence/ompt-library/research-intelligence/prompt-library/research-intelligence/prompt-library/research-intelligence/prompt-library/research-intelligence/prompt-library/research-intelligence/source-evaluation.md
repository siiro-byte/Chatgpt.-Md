Source Evaluation & Evidence Quality

Status: Draft

Purpose

Evaluate the credibility, reliability and usefulness of information sources.

Prompt

Act as a senior research librarian and evidence-quality analyst.

Evaluate the following source or collection of sources:

SUBJECT: [Insert research subject]

SOURCES: [Insert links, documents or references]

RESEARCH PURPOSE: [Explain what the information will be used for]

Evaluation process

1. Identify the original publisher, author or issuing organisation.
2. Establish the publication and last-updated dates.
3. Determine whether the source provides primary or secondary evidence.
4. Examine the author's expertise and institutional credibility.
5. Identify the evidence supporting its claims.
6. Check whether methods, data and references are disclosed.
7. Examine potential conflicts of interest or commercial incentives.
8. Assess whether the information is independently corroborated.
9. Identify outdated information, missing context or misleading presentation.
10. Determine whether the source is appropriate for the intended decision.

Required output

- Source identification
- Publisher and author
- Publication and update dates
- Primary or secondary classification
- Evidence quality
- Credibility assessment
- Independence assessment
- Potential bias
- Corroborating sources
- Weaknesses and limitations
- Recommended use
- Overall confidence

Rating framework

- High reliability
- Moderate reliability
- Limited reliability
- Unreliable
- Insufficient information

Explain the reasoning behind each rating.

Rules

- Do not assume an official source is automatically correct.
- Do not confuse popularity with credibility.
- Do not treat multiple outlets repeating one original report as independent confirmation.
- Distinguish source credibility from the accuracy of an individual claim.
- Never invent author credentials or institutional affiliations.

Final instruction: Assess the evidence, not just the reputation of the publisher.
