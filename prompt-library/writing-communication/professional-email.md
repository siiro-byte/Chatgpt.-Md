# Professional Email Drafter

## Purpose
Draft clear, concise and appropriately toned professional emails that achieve a specific communication objective and make the recipient's next action easy to understand.

## When to use
Use for business correspondence, requests, follow-ups, introductions, updates, escalations, negotiations, apologies, confirmations and professional responses.

## Required inputs
- Communication objective
- Recipient and relationship/context
- Key facts and relevant previous correspondence
- Desired action or decision from the recipient
- Deadline or timing, if applicable
- Desired tone and level of formality
- Sender identity/sign-off, if known

## Instructions
1. Identify the email's primary purpose and the one action or decision the recipient should take.
2. Establish the recipient relationship and appropriate tone: formal, neutral, warm, persuasive, firm, apologetic or urgent as requested.
3. Extract relevant facts, dates, commitments and context from the supplied material. Do not invent names, email addresses, attachments, approvals, promises or deadlines.
4. Write a specific subject line that signals the topic or requested action without exaggeration.
5. Open with a natural greeting and state the purpose early. Include only the context the recipient needs to understand the request.
6. Organise the body around the key message, supporting details and requested action. Use short paragraphs and bullets for multiple items.
7. Make the requested action explicit. If timing matters, use only a supplied deadline; otherwise use a neutral request for the recipient to advise.
8. Keep the tone respectful and direct. Avoid unnecessary apologies, aggressive language, excessive pleasantries, vague urgency and manipulative phrasing.
9. Close with a suitable next step and sign-off. Use a placeholder for missing sender details rather than fabricating them.
10. Check that the draft does not disclose unnecessary confidential information and that all factual statements are supported by the supplied context.
11. If important information is missing, provide the best safe draft with clearly marked placeholders and list the missing details separately.

## Expected output
- Subject line
- Complete email draft with greeting, body, clear action and sign-off
- Placeholders for missing details
- Brief note on any assumptions or information needed before sending

## Quality checks
- One clear purpose and recipient action
- Subject accurately reflects the message
- Tone suits the relationship and situation
- Facts and commitments are grounded in supplied context
- No invented addresses, names, attachments or deadlines
- Concise, readable and respectful
- Confidential information is limited to what is necessary
