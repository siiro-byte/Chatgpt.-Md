# Strategic Options Memo

## Purpose
Structure a consequential business decision, generate genuinely distinct strategic options, compare trade-offs and recommend a course of action with clear assumptions, risks and decision triggers.

## When to use
Use when choosing between growth paths, market entry, partnerships, investment, product priorities, operating models, expansion, repositioning or other material strategic alternatives.

## Required inputs
- Decision owner and decision deadline
- Desired outcome and time horizon
- Current situation and reason a decision is needed
- Available options already under consideration
- Financial, operational, legal and capability constraints
- Relevant market/customer evidence
- Risk tolerance and reversibility considerations

## Instructions
1. State the decision as a clear question. Define who decides, by when, and what success means over the relevant time horizon.
2. Establish the baseline: current position, strategic objective, constraints, assets, capabilities and external conditions. Separate facts from assumptions.
3. Identify the criteria that matter to the decision. Include strategic fit, customer value, financial upside, cost, time to impact, execution capacity, risk, reversibility and opportunity cost where relevant.
4. Generate a small set of distinct options, including maintaining the current course where useful. Avoid presenting minor variations as separate strategies.
5. For each option describe the strategic logic, target customer/market, value created, required capabilities, resources, dependencies, likely sequence and what must be true for it to work.
6. Compare options against the same criteria. Use quantitative estimates only when inputs support them; otherwise use transparent qualitative ranges and confidence levels.
7. Examine downside scenarios, second-order effects, competitor/customer responses, operational bottlenecks and risks of inaction.
8. Assess reversibility and option value. Identify low-regret moves, staged commitments and decisions that can be delayed until better evidence arrives.
9. Identify critical assumptions and the evidence that would confirm or disconfirm each. State which unknowns could change the ranking.
10. Recommend one option and explain why it dominates under the stated objectives and constraints. Name the strongest alternative and why it was not selected.
11. Translate the recommendation into staged actions, owners or roles, milestones, leading indicators and review points. Do not invent named owners or dates.
12. Define decision triggers: what evidence would cause the organisation to scale, adapt, pause or reverse course.

## Expected output
- Decision question, owner and deadline
- Objective, baseline and constraints
- Decision criteria and weighting rationale
- Distinct strategic options
- Comparative assessment and trade-off table
- Key risks, dependencies and scenarios
- Critical assumptions and evidence gaps
- Recommended option and rationale
- Staged implementation roadmap
- Review points and decision triggers

## Quality checks
- Options are meaningfully distinct and feasible
- Criteria reflect the actual decision
- Comparisons use consistent assumptions
- Uncertainty and evidence gaps are visible
- Risks include downside and opportunity cost
- Recommendation follows from stated criteria
- Implementation steps are proportionate and actionable
- Conditions that would change the recommendation are explicit
