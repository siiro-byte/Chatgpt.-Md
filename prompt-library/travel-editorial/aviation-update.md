# Aviation & Transport Update

## Purpose
Verify an airline, airport, route or transport development and explain its operational meaning for passengers.

## When to use
Use for delays, cancellations, diversions, suspensions, route launches, schedule changes, airport disruption, strikes and transport interruptions.

## Required inputs
- Operator, airport, route or service
- Reported development or source link
- Date/time window and service number if known
- Journey or connection context
- Intended audience and format

## Instructions
1. Identify the operator, facility and exact route. Establish whether the issue affects one service, several services, an airport or a wider network.
2. Check primary sources: operator alerts, airport status, aviation/transport authority notices and official statements.
3. Verify event date, local time zone, affected service numbers, route direction, duration and latest status.
4. Use precise terms: delayed, cancelled, diverted, suspended, rerouted, restricted, resumed, planned or launched. Do not describe an announced route as operating.
5. Do not generalise a single flight disruption to an entire airline or airport.
6. State passenger consequences only when supported. Direct passengers to the operator for booking-specific decisions.
7. For route launches, verify start date, airports, carrier, frequency and ticket availability; distinguish announcement from first flight.
8. Record source URLs and update times, flag stale status, and state what travellers should check now.

## Expected output
- Headline and current status
- What changed and when
- Affected operator, airport, route and services
- Confirmed passenger impact
- Practical traveller action
- Uncertain details
- Dated operator/authority sources

## Quality checks
- Dates and time zones clear
- Scope not overstated
- Operational terms used correctly
- Planned vs operating service distinguished
- Passenger advice supported by evidence
