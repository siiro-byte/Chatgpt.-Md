# Voiceover Script & Audio Direction

## Purpose
Write narration that sounds natural when spoken, fits a defined duration and audience, and communicates the supplied message accurately. Include delivery, pacing, pronunciation and recording guidance.

## When to use
Use for videos, explainers, advertisements, travel content, documentaries, presentations, social clips, audio guides and narrated tutorials.

## Required inputs
- Purpose, audience and desired listener response
- Source facts, approved copy or reference material
- Target duration and approximate speaking pace
- Platform and content format
- Desired voice, tone, language and accent preference
- Required names, terminology, pronunciation and disclosures
- Visual context or scene order, if the narration accompanies video

## Instructions
1. Identify the listener, objective, format and target duration. Confirm whether the script is narration-only, presenter speech, dialogue or a mix.
2. Establish a realistic word budget using the requested delivery pace. State the assumed words per minute and allow pauses for emphasis or visual transitions.
3. Extract the essential message and supporting facts. Preserve dates, names, figures, qualifiers and uncertainty. Do not add unsupported claims or invented anecdotes.
4. Structure the narration for listening, not reading: strong opening, clear progression, short spoken sentences, transitions and a purposeful ending.
5. Use conversational language and concrete wording. Remove dense clauses, unexplained acronyms, awkward punctuation and phrases that are difficult to say aloud.
6. Mark pauses, emphasis, pace changes, emotional tone and pronunciation only where useful. Avoid over-directing every sentence.
7. If the script is tied to visuals, align narration with the scene order and identify moments where the visuals should carry the message without speech.
8. Keep calls to action clear and appropriate. Include required disclosures or source attribution when the content requires them.
9. Estimate duration from the final word count and stated pace. If too long, trim repetition before removing essential context or caveats.
10. Provide alternate hooks or endings only when they offer a meaningful creative choice. Do not create multiple versions unnecessarily.
11. Review the script aloud mentally for rhythm, breath length, clarity and pronunciation risks. Flag names or terms requiring confirmation.

## Expected output
- Creative direction and delivery style
- Complete voiceover script
- Word count and estimated duration with assumed speaking pace
- Pause, emphasis and pronunciation notes
- Optional visual/sound alignment cues
- Source/disclosure notes where required
- Recording and final-edit checklist

## Quality checks
- Script fits the target duration at the stated pace
- Language sounds natural when spoken
- Essential facts and caveats are preserved
- No unsupported claims or invented details
- Pronunciation and delivery notes are useful
- Narration complements rather than repeats visuals
- Ending and call to action are clear
